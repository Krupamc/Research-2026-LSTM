Results:

Models were choosen for the final prediction setup based on accuracy (lowest MAE). In the future, there will be a option to choose what model to use but until then, the models will be the default.

Linear regression produced the lowest errors for wind speed and wind gust speed, with MAEs of 0.56 and 0.96 mph, respectively. The naive persistence model performed best for wind direction, with an MAE of 0.73 direction bins. Each of the 16 bins represented 22.5°, corresponding to an approximate angular error of 16.5°. Predicted directions within the onshore range of NNE through S were classified as onshore. This classification produced an MAE of 0.13

Analysis:

The MAE values highlight the strengths and weaknesses of each model. Linear regression performed best predicting values that have a linear relationship between the input and output. The naive persistence models performed best for wind direction, suggesting that recent direction was a useful one-hour forecasting baseline in this dataset.

The relative performance of the models differed for wind direction. The naive persistence model produced a MAE of 0.73 compared to linear regression’s 3.82. Though not as accurate as expected, this direction prediction is accurate enough for recreational boaters and paddlers out on the water. Classifying the predicted wind directions as onshore or offshore produced an MAE of 0.13, corresponding to approximately 87% classification accuracy. Even though it came so close to LSTM’s 0.14, this approach was surprisingly more accurate than using any model

The rarity of upwelling events during the end of the dataset made prediction difficult for all models. There was only one upwelling event in the last 10% of the dataset (September 18, 2026) as most upwelling events occurred in the summer months. No model predicted that event. Thus, the models remain untested for upwelling (Figure 14).

Conclusion:

Although the LSTM did not perform as hypothesized, the simpler models produced useful next-hour predictions of wind speed, wind gust speed, and wind direction. Linear regression achieved the lowest wind-speed and gust-speed errors, while persistence achieved the lowest direction error. Predicted wind directions were used to classify onshore conditions. The LSTM did not outperform the strongest baseline for these targets.

These results suggest that a locally focused forecasting program could provide useful information for recreational boaters and paddlers. However, the absence of labeled upwelling events in the evaluated test targets prevented assessment of event-detection performance. The program and website provide a foundation for further development. Future work could evaluate additional model designs, improve predictions of rare events, and expand the existing website’s forecasting capabilities.
