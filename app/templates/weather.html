Weather!

Here is some background on the weather behind this project and why is it important.

Onshore winds (also known as sea breezes) are winds that travel from the ocean to the land. Onshore winds are created from the differential heating between land and water. As warm air over land rises, cooler, denser air over the water moves inland to replace it, producing an onshore wind.

Onshore winds can influence the atmosphere of any costal area by lowering local temperature by approximately 15° Fahrenheit and even possibly affecting air quality and pollution levels. Onshore effects can spread far inland and can also create sea breeze fronts. Sea Breeze fronts are familar to cold fronts and often assocated with lines of storms. In relation, these fronts can even create thunder storms!

Upwelling:

Along the east coast of the United States, specfically New Jersey (NJ), persistent South, Southeast, or West onshore winds push warm water of the surface of bays, estuaries, and coastal waters (also known as Ekman Transport) to initate the upwelling process. Upwelling is the mixing of the warmer top water and denser cold water to cool the entire body of water.

In Barnegat Bay, NJ, high tides bring in cold water from ocean upwelling, while low tides carry it out of the bay through the three inlets: Manasquan, Barnegat Light, and Little Egg Harbor.

The temperature difference between the bay and land is amplified, leading to stronger gusts of onshore winds, positive input loop of more upwelling and a larger temperature difference. The stronger gusts of winds can create waves strong enough to "swamp" small watercraft such as kayaks, jet skis, and even small boats.

Traditional Weather Forecasts:

Wind forecasts created for large areas may generalize wind patterns over broad areas and periods, missing key factors such as sudden upwelling events or sharp land-sea temperature gradients that can form in a matter of hours. With these traditional models, small craft advisories or wind alerts may not be issues in time. Boaters may encounter dangerous conditions without sufficent warning.
